# Leakage and selection effects

Only numeric measurements in the feature allowlist enter flow models. The importer excludes identifiers, timestamps, attack category, class and source partition from training features. All preprocessing, anomaly reference distributions and classifier parameters derive from training. Fit calibration maps and set review-quantile thresholds using separate calibration data. The final test set supplies outcomes and retrospective feature permutation analysis. No feature selection or refitting follows those attributions.

The projection onto 19 features creates duplicate and inconsistent observations. Excluding conflicts and keeping one record per distinct observation changes the prevalence, attack mix and cost interpretation. A vector repeated thousands of times receives the same weight as a vector seen once. We estimate behaviour on distinct unambiguous observations. The resulting false-alarm rate must not be multiplied by raw enterprise flow volume as an operational forecast.

The import removes raw test-vector overlap with the training source. Numeric clipping and conversion can still collapse distinct inputs, as documented in the transformed-overlap audit. Exact deduplication cannot control all related-host or related-scenario dependence. No random row shuffle creates an independent site. The sequence analysis verifies identical token-sequence hashes across partitions; per-directory weak labels can include benign processes.

A test-derived incident packet is a retrospective diagnostic. Its balanced confusion-cell composition is deliberately unrepresentative of deployment prevalence. The developer-authored investigation key and scoring rules share design assumptions. Human inter-rater agreement and analyst productivity require independent evaluation.

The independent CSV audit counted 132,175 distinct numeric vectors and 132,175 distinct IDs across all full-cohort UNSW partitions. Converting distinct vectors through the trained model's preprocessing can still collapse values. The separate 11-feature NetFlow v2 study audited all raw binary labels against category names, then used a 50,000-row priority sample per corpus. The source sample had 5,956 distinct projected vectors and the target 13,039. Removing six source and 137 target binary-label conflicts plus 13 target/source overlaps changes the score population. Full raw attack prevalence was 3.98% in the NetFlow source and 11.95% in the target. In the scored cohorts it was 18.41% and 23.99%. AP and Brier differences therefore reflect prevalence as well as ranking and probability changes. The ROC-AUC also fell across corpora. Neither collection establishes deployment transportability.
